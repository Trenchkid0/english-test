B1 · 3 MENITUrban life

Why some cities are making room for quiet

A short report on traffic, public space, and the unexpected value of slower streets.

Several city centres have tested car-free hours at weekends. At first, shop owners feared that fewer cars would mean fewer customers. However, surveys in three districts found that visitors stayed longer and spent slightly more when streets were easier to walk through.

The trials did not remove traffic entirely. Instead, they moved deliveries to early mornings and gave buses clearer routes. City planners say the result is not a single solution, but a useful reminder that public space can serve more than one purpose.

Useful wordsdistrictdeliveriespublic space
B2 · 4 MENITLearning science

Memory improves when effort is visible

Why recalling an answer can teach more than rereading the page.

Researchers often distinguish between recognition and retrieval. Recognition feels familiar when a learner sees an answer again; retrieval requires the learner to produce it without help. The second process usually feels harder, but that difficulty is part of its value.

Short quizzes, spaced review, and explaining an idea aloud all create small retrieval challenges. They also give useful feedback: a wrong answer shows exactly what needs another pass.

Useful wordsretrievalspaced reviewchallenge
